# Eden Industrial Systems — Agent Direction

This file defines the active website direction for Eden Industrial Systems (EIS). Treat it as a governing instruction for future website work in this repository.

## Company Positioning

Eden Industrial Systems is positioned as a **service-first industrial engineering business focused on custom production tooling and practical automation for manufacturers**.

Primary public capabilities:

1. **Assembly and press-fit tooling** — leading application
2. **Inspection fixtures**
3. **Production workholding**
4. **Operator-assist equipment and small custom automation**

Core positioning statement:

> Eden Industrial Systems designs custom assembly fixtures, inspection workholding, press-fit tooling, production fixtures, and practical operator-assist equipment around the parts, machines, and people already on the customer's floor.

Preferred company descriptor:

> **Custom production tooling and practical automation**

The website should remain focused on real manufacturing problems rather than broad claims about automation capability.

## Current Commercial Direction

Assembly and press-fit tooling is the current lead application for EIS.

Relevant application examples may include:

- Bushing insertion
- Bearing installation
- Pin and dowel pressing
- Seal installation
- Sleeve or insert installation
- Component seating
- Mechanical staking applications
- Alignment and support during assembly
- Controlled insertion depth
- Part-location and poka-yoke features
- Changeable nests
- Integration with an existing arbor, pneumatic, hydraulic, or servo press

Do **not** imply that Eden currently sells a standardized automatic bushing press or any universal press system.

Do **not** design the public website around one hypothetical machine. The company remains a custom tooling and mechanical systems business.

Customer-specific cut-to-length or material-positioning systems may be discussed as possible operator-assist applications, but Eden does **not** currently sell a standardized or universal cut-to-length machine.

## Press-Fit Sales and Qualification Philosophy

Website copy, forms, calls to action, and project-intake flows should support the following commercial workflow:

1. Identify a recurring manufacturing problem.
2. Perform a short initial qualification.
3. Determine whether deeper discovery is justified.
4. Collect relevant part, process, press, quality, volume, and cycle information.
5. Quantify the business problem using customer-provided or observed data.
6. Select the smallest useful project scope.
7. Conduct technical, commercial, confidentiality, and safety screening.
8. Proceed to a paid engineering or tooling project only when the opportunity is qualified.

The website should encourage prospects to share the **part, current process, production volume, existing equipment, and production problem** rather than asking them to choose a machine.

Preferred CTA language:

- Request a Project Review
- Discuss Your Application
- Review Your Process
- Discuss an Inspection Fixture
- Submit Project for Review

Avoid ecommerce-style language such as:

- Buy Now
- Order a Machine
- Get Instant Pricing
- Guaranteed Quote

---

## Qualification Principles

Eden should prioritize opportunities where several of the following are present:

- Recurring production volume
- Manual alignment or repetitive handling
- Existing press or production equipment already available
- Quality, scrap, rework, seating, or repeatability concerns
- Measurable cycle time or labor content
- Ergonomic or operator-loading issues
- Multiple related assemblies or part families
- Customer access to drawings, models, samples, photos, or machine information
- A defined internal process owner or manufacturing champion
- A realistic commercial budget and purchasing path

Do not treat production volume alone as proof of a good project. A high-volume process that already works well may have little value to improve.

Do not confuse technical interest with buying intent.

Do not provide substantial unpaid production-ready engineering merely to win a lead.

## Press-Fit Project Levels

When describing potential project scope, use the following framework where useful.

### Level 1 — Fixture Improvement

Typical scope:

- Existing press remains
- Operator loads the parts
- Custom nest
- Better locating and alignment
- Insertion tooling
- Positive depth control where practical
- Mechanical loading or unloading improvements

This is a preferred early EIS project type.

### Level 2 — Assisted Pressing

Potential scope:

- Operator loads the parts
- Sensors may verify presence or orientation
- Press sequencing may be controlled
- Automatic return or ejection may be added
- Pass/fail indication may be added

Electrical, controls, safety, and integration responsibilities must be explicitly scoped.

### Level 3 — Monitored Pressing

Potential scope:

- Force monitoring
- Position monitoring
- Defined pass/fail limits
- Recipe selection
- Data collection or traceability
- Reject handling

This usually requires qualified controls, press, and integration partners.

### Level 4 — Automatic Assembly

Potential scope:

- Automatic part feeding
- Orientation
- Loading
- Pressing
- Verification
- Ejection
- Counting or traceability

Do not promote Level 4 as a standard Eden offering. It should only be considered when volume, economics, technical requirements, customer budget, safety responsibilities, and partner capabilities support it.

## Safety and Powered-Motion Rules

Any public description of powered presses or automated motion must avoid implying that the mechanical concept alone makes a machine safe.

Do not claim:

- Safety certified
- Fully guarded
- OSHA compliant
- ANSI compliant
- Risk assessed
- Validated safety system

unless supporting project-specific evidence exists and the claim has been approved.

For powered systems, responsibility for controls, electrical work, guarding, risk assessment, installation, commissioning, and final machine-safety review must be project-specific.

Never encourage removal or bypass of existing safety devices.

- Contact name

- Email
- Phone
- Project type
- Current process
- Production problem
- Part or material

- Existing equipment or machine
- Annual or batch volume
- Current cycle time if known
- Required tolerance or quality requirement
- Scrap, rework, ergonomic, access, or handling concern

- Delivery timeframe
- Quantity
- Additional requirements

- Name
- Company
- Email
- Project type
